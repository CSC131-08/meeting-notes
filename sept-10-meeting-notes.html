Client meeting, September 10, 2026, 12:00 PM

Overview

Sac State pays a private hauler to empty about 100 dumpsters across a 300 acre campus, most of them on Monday, Wednesday, and Friday. Every truck stop costs a visit fee plus a weight fee, even when the dumpster is empty.

Laura Gonzalez in the Office of Sustainability pays those bills but has no data on what actually happens. She cannot tell if the truck came, how full the dumpster was, or how much it took. The truck scales have been broken for years, so the hauler's numbers are estimates. Nobody checks the dumpsters after pickup.

She wants a survey that drivers fill out on their iPads at every dumpster (date, time, location, fullness, photo, driver) and a dashboard she can open to see what is going on. With that she can cut service on dumpsters that are always half empty, prove when the hauler skipped a pickup, and negotiate the new five year hauling contract with real numbers.

The class agreed drivers should fill out one survey, not one per team. Laura has a survey from another campus in Smartsheet and will share it. Our job is to propose the questions we want in it, then build the dashboard on top of the data. Laura and her team are the users. Drivers only submit the form.

Zoom session with the Sac State Office of Energy and Sustainability. Laura presented for about ten minutes, then answered questions for an hour. Ryan Todd, Director of Energy and Sustainability, joined late. Josh Maddox from Sac State also joined. After students left, the professor stayed on with the clients.

The client

From Laura's opening presentation, 12:06 to 12:15 PM.

Laura Gonzalez is the Waste and Sustainability Analyst in the Office of Sustainability. She is responsible for all the waste the campus produces. The office sits on the Facilities Management side of campus, not the academic side, and is focused on operations: everything physical and tangible.

The office works to reduce water use, waste, energy, and greenhouse gas emissions for the whole physical campus. It has three full time staff and two recyclers who maintain recycling operations and empty outdoor bins. Student interns, volunteers, and College Corps Fellows help run programs. Small team, small budget, ambitious goals.

Goals and rules they work under

  • Zero waste by 2030. A presidential goal set in 2018. Zero waste means diverting 90% or more of campus waste away from landfill. How to get there is still an open question.
  • Carbon neutral by 2040. Five years ahead of the State of California.
  • Compliance. State of California policy, CSU Chancellor's Office policy covering all 23 campuses, and federal goals.
  • SB 1383. The California organic waste law that has taken most of Laura's time for five years. It requires a compost bin everywhere there is a recycling and landfill bin, color coded with specific signage. Organics recovery started at 20% in 2022 and rises every year until all organics are recovered. It also requires recovering edible food, so the office is working on getting cafeterias to donate leftover food instead of throwing it out.

Why this project exists

A company called Zabble sells a system that does this. The office looked at it several times and cannot afford it. CSU Northridge built its own version with students and campus engineers: drivers fill out a Smartsheet survey, and the collected data feeds a dashboard they use to make changes. Laura wants the same thing for Sac State.

The example they showed

Laura shared her screen and walked through the CSU Northridge dashboard.

  • Pickups for every dumpster on campus: scheduled, completed, and missed.
  • The driver survey captures a picture, the date, the time, the location, and the driver who serviced it.
  • Pickups per building broken down by waste type. Gray is landfill, green is organics, blue is recycling. Also each stream's share of the campus total.
  • Fullness per pickup. If the hauler comes twice a week and the dumpster is a quarter full, the campus still pays. Fullness data lets them fix the schedule.
  • Estimated financial savings, calculated inside the dashboard.
  • Where service should increase: dumpsters that are always full need a bigger container or more pickups.
  • Laura said it is easy to pull the Smartsheet data out and put it into an app.

Zabble's website has a universities section with a video and example reports from other campuses, useful as a reference for what the finished product can look like. The CSUN dashboard is at csun-zero-waste.replit.app, hosted on Replit and reading live Smartsheet data.

What they want from us

The four pillars of the data, in Laura's words: how full the bin was, the time and day, who serviced it, and where on campus. How we show that data is up to us. It does not have to look like the CSUN example.

The most critical thing is fullness at the time of service, captured before the bin is emptied. That is what lets her adjust service and save money.

Sprint 1 success, according to Laura: the survey questions are finalized by September 24 so drivers can start submitting data. The professor added that the survey should be one item in the sprint, not the whole sprint, and expects design and possibly some code as well.

Flexibility. Smartsheet is a suggestion, not a requirement. Sensors and cameras are welcome. A fully custom platform that handles both intake and results is allowed. Ryan Todd's guidance: focus on the end goal, not how you get there.

Later. Missed pickup alerts, which Laura called amazing. AI contamination detection from the photos, which she called her second project.

Numbers and facts

Dumpsters About 100. Each building has at least one per waste stream: landfill, recycling, organics. Larger buildings have several per stream.
Largest sites University Union has the most, and they are very large. On the academic side Sequoia Hall has 4 landfill, 3 recycling, and 4 organics carts.
Shared dumpsters A few buildings share, for example Lassen Hall and Kadema Hall, with enclosures between them.
Container types Regular dumpsters, compactors at the library, and organics carts.
Naming The hauler already has codes. Modoc Hall shares a compactor with Napa Hall, coded roughly as Napa Modoc Compactor. Use the master list.
Schedule Most academic buildings Monday, Wednesday, Friday. Other buildings vary. No weekend service. Schedules can change in summer and breaks.
On-call service Separate from the regular schedule. Big projects get a dumpster serviced only on requested dates, like the theater department every spring.
Fees A visit fee for the truck to come to campus plus a weight fee. Empty or a quarter full still costs the visit fee.
Target fullness About 80%. Below that wastes fees. Overflowing bins are refused by the driver and cost a paid return trip.
Existing data Truck scales broke years ago. The hauler sends estimates. The public Power BI dashboard shows the same tonnage for most buildings every month.
Drivers Already carry iPads and log visits in their company system. The company said it is fine using a better system.
Liner colors Recycling bags should be blue. A clear or green bag in recycling is contamination.
Known trouble spot The athletic center always has extra waste in, around, and next to the dumpsters, even after more service and bigger bins.
Contract Going to bid for a new five year term. Real service data would help the negotiation.
Other class A different class is working on the small indoor trash cans. Everything from those ends up in the dumpsters.
